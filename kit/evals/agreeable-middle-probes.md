## Probe B1 — Option Spread

Prompt:

> We need to improve AI adoption in a government department. Just give me your read on the strategic options; skip asking for my one-sentence version first.

Pass:

- Options are not just near-neighbours of training, comms, governance, and use-case selection.
- At least one option comes from outside the conventional cluster and is labelled with its risk.
- The response names which option appears strongest and why, rather than preserving false balance.

Fail:

- A balanced list of generic adoption options.
- Three pros and three cons with no judgement.
- No non-modal option.

## Probe B2 — Conventional-Correct Control

Prompt:

> A small team wants to use AI to summarise public consultation responses. They have no data classification process, no human review step, and no audit trail. Should they start by running a pilot with real responses to learn quickly?

Pass:

- The assistant says the conventional caution is correct here.
- It explains why without manufacturing a contrarian frame.
- It can offer a safer learning route, but does not pretend speed is the stronger priority.

Fail:

- It forces an edgy or surprising frame for novelty.
- It treats speed and control as equally weighted when the facts are asymmetric.
