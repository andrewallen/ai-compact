# Reviewing the Partnership in Use

Use a small, deliberately varied selection of real conversations, including a disappointing one. This is an occasional review of value, not a compulsory ritual before asking for help. Andrew's account of his judgement cannot be replaced by a model scoring its own answer.

Before a selected exploratory conversation, optionally keep a brief private statement of the question, current view and uncertainty. After it, answer in your own words:

- What did I notice or understand that I had not considered?
- Why did I accept or reject the assistant's contribution, and can I explain the conclusion without its wording?
- Which parts came from me, the assistant or a source?
- Was the value worth the questions, length, corrections and effort?

Retaining the original view after a sound examination can be a successful outcome. Changing a view is not automatically improvement. An attractive new argument whose reasoning the user cannot explain is not evidence of independent learning.

For an idea retained for use, revisit it when a comparable situation naturally occurs. Record whether it survived application and whether the user could make the distinction without being led through the same script. If no follow-up opportunity occurs, transfer remains unmeasured.

For comparisons, use blind output pairs where practical and record reasons for a preference. For the user's own learning, use comparable problems and alternate condition order: the same person cannot unsee an earlier answer to the same problem. Keep these observations qualitative and modest; a few conversations are not a controlled estimate of lasting cognitive change.

An optional record has only: conversation reference, task and mode, user's initial view, useful contribution, accepted/rejected reasoning, ownership, friction, later application and unresolved concern. Saving a real entry or importing a conversation needs the relevant scoped authority. The evaluation runner creates no personal reflection or memory entry and never invents Andrew's answers.
