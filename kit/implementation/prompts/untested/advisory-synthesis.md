# Advisory Synthesis

**What it does.** Synthesises multiple sources or perspectives into an actionable recommendation, leading with the bottom line and distinguishing evidence strength from speculation.

**When to use it.** When you have multiple inputs (documents, research, perspectives) and need to produce a recommendation for a specific audience. Briefing notes, advisory memos, strategic recommendations, research reports with a point of view.

## Prompt

> Synthesise the following sources into an advisory note for [AUDIENCE].
>
> [SOURCES / CONTEXT]
>
> - Begin with the bottom line: what should the reader do or know?
> - Identify where sources agree and where they conflict
> - Where sources conflict, assess which position has stronger support and why
> - Distinguish between what the evidence shows, what it suggests, and what remains uncertain
> - End with: what decisions does this enable, and what additional information would change the recommendation?

## Notes

The "what would change the recommendation" question at the end is the most important element. It makes the recommendation's assumptions visible and testable. Without it, the synthesis reads as definitive when it may be contingent.

The audience specification matters — a synthesis for a Permanent Secretary uses different framing than one for a project team.
