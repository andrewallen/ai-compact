# Multi-Perspective Exploration

**What it does.** Explores a complex issue through three distinct expert perspectives, surfacing each one's core claim, strongest evidence, and blind spots — then synthesising convergence and genuine conflict.

**When to use it.** When you need to understand an issue from multiple valid viewpoints before forming a position. Strategic questions, policy dilemmas, technology choices, organisational design decisions. A formalised version of the expansion function.

## Prompt

> Explore [TOPIC] from three distinct perspectives:
> 1. [PERSPECTIVE_1 — e.g., an economist focused on incentives]
> 2. [PERSPECTIVE_2 — e.g., a social scientist focused on institutional behaviour]
> 3. [PERSPECTIVE_3 — e.g., a practitioner with 20 years of operational experience]
>
> For each perspective:
> - State the core claim this expert would make
> - Identify the strongest evidence supporting their view
> - Identify what this perspective tends to overlook
>
> Then synthesise: where do these perspectives converge? Where do they genuinely conflict? What does the convergence suggest about what is most likely true?

## Notes

Choose perspectives that represent genuinely different analytical frames, not just different conclusions from the same frame. The value is in seeing the issue through lenses that would not naturally co-occur in one mind.

The "what this perspective overlooks" requirement prevents the model from building three versions of the same argument.
