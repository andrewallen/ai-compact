# Position Development

**What it does.** Builds an evidence-based working thesis on a topic, testing its foundations and defining the conditions under which it should be revised.

**When to use it.** When you are developing a view on a topic you engage with deeply and want to test its structure before committing to it. Thought leadership preparation, strategic position-taking, argument construction.

## Prompt

> I am developing a position on [TOPIC]. My current thinking is:
> [THESIS / WORKING VIEW]
>
> Help me stress-test this:
> 1. State the strongest version of this position — sharper than I have stated it
> 2. What evidence would need to be true for this position to hold with confidence?
> 3. What is the most credible objection a thoughtful sceptic would raise?
> 4. Under what conditions should I revise this position?
> 5. What am I not seeing that someone outside my frame would?

## Notes

Maps directly to "How I Build Positions" in `kit/constitution/01-calibration.md` — working theses held with conviction, actively wanting them challenged. Question 5 invokes the expansion function explicitly.

The "strongest version" request in question 1 often surfaces the real argument underneath a softer formulation. The revision conditions in question 4 make the position falsifiable.
