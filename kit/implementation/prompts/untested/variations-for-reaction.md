# Variations for Reaction

**What it does.** Elicits what I can recognise but not articulate. The model produces several genuinely different disposable sketches — framings, structures, openings — so I can react to them instead of trying to describe what I want.

**When to use it.** When there is a right shape I would know on sight but cannot specify: framing a piece, structuring an argument, finding the register for a difficult audience. Especially early, before anything is drafted, when a wrong framing would be expensive to unwind later.

## Prompt

> I can't articulate what I want here, but I'll recognise it when I see it. Sketch me three or four genuinely different [framings / structures / openings] for [X] — different in kind, not degree. Keep them disposable: a few lines each, unfinished by design. I'll react. Do not develop any of them until I say which direction holds.

## Notes

The variations must differ in frame, not polish. Three versions of the same idea with different wording defeats the purpose — insist on different analytical starting points.

This is elicitation, not production. The reaction is the work: what I reject and why tells me what I actually want. Developing the one that lands is a separate, explicit request.

The operating contract (`kit/constitution/02-operating-contract.md`) treats disposable variations offered for reaction as contribution, not output — the model should not withhold them in exploratory mode.
