# What Good Looks Like

**What it does.** Before judging or commissioning work in an unfamiliar domain, has the model teach me the evaluative standard — the dimensions on which quality varies and how someone who knows the domain tells good from merely plausible.

**When to use it.** Whenever I need to assess work, options, or advice in territory where I do not yet know what good looks like. Run it before asking for the work or the variations, not after — options are useless if I cannot judge them.

## Prompt

> I need to judge [work / options / a decision] in [domain], and I don't yet know what good looks like there. Teach me the evaluative standard: the dimensions on which quality varies, what separates good from plausible, and the failure modes a non-expert accepts without noticing. Don't evaluate anything yet — equip me to.

## Notes

The quality bar in the operating contract assumes I can recognise good. In unfamiliar domains that assumption fails silently: the failure mode is accepting competent-looking work I had no basis to assess. This prompt closes that gap by acquiring the standard first.

Order matters. Standard first, then judgement — running this after seeing the work anchors the standard to what was produced.
