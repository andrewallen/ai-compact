# Examples

Targets to match in feel, not templates to replicate.

## One scenario across every register

Scenario: after a public-sector workshop, Andrew wants to lock in a zero-legacy deep-dive and bring two colleagues in to lead it. The four communications below share the voice DNA; the register flexes. The documentation example further down shows the contrasting deliverable.

### Counter-examples — do not imitate

These are failure examples for detector use. They are included to show what flattened or AI-written versions look like. Do not treat them as targets.

**Flattened broadcast / framing:**
> Great momentum from Thursday's workshop — this was not just a discussion, it was a real opportunity to unlock the next phase of our partnership. The team did an excellent job of bringing the conversation to life, and now we need to leverage that energy to drive impact going forward.

Tells: praise reflex, "not just X, it was Y", `unlock` / `leverage` / `impact`, abstract momentum tail, generic corporate uplift.

**Flattened customer email:**
> Hi [Name], I hope this email finds you well. Following our productive workshop, I wanted to reach out to explore the possibility of scheduling a follow-up session on zero-legacy transformation. This would enable us to delve deeper into the key themes and ensure alignment on next steps.

Tells: boilerplate opener, "wanted to reach out", `transformation`, `delve`, `alignment`, no concrete reason why the follow-up matters.

**Internal Teams (group chat):**
> Morning all - quick one off the back of Thursday. Real momentum from the department on legacy, so I want to capitalise while it's warm. Proposing a zero-legacy deep-dive in the next fortnight - [Colleague A] and [Colleague B] to lead, given they carried it well on the day. ## Authored register — longer-form feel

**Layered narrative — thought piece register**

*"Enterprise account planning, as commonly practiced, has a structural flaw. It describes organisations accurately and says nothing useful.*

*The standard approach gathers published strategy, maps organisational structure, catalogues stated priorities, and aligns the vendor's products to those priorities. The output is a document that is correct on the day it is written and largely irrelevant within months — superseded by a machinery of government change, a spending review announcement, a leadership departure, or the quiet deprioritisation of an initiative that was never going to be funded in the first place.*

*This is not a competence problem. The people who build account plans are often deeply knowledgeable about their customers. The problem is methodological."*

A provocative opening. A paragraph that builds the case by describing the conventional approach and why it fails. Then a reframe. Each paragraph earns the next.

**Argument register — building a case**

Conventional explanation first — stated fairly, not as a straw man. Then the reframe: the conventional explanation is treating symptoms, and the real mechanism is different. A specific, counterintuitive claim grounded in evidence. Then the prescription that follows logically. Confident without being absolute. The argument builds so that the conclusion feels earned, not asserted.

**Practical register — direct, human, no ceremony**

*"Good afternoon all — thank you for attending the workshop last week. A lot of ground was covered, along with a number of follow up actions — please find attached a report written up using researcher on the transcripts captured during the session.*

*I've not checked everything so please make sure if there are any glaring mis-interpretations from the discussion, but my plan is to use this to create some additional supporting assets between us as we work together going forward."*

Direct, honest about limitations, moves immediately to next steps.

## Documentation register — write-up feel

A workshop output opening — neutral and structured, conveying understanding, not my personal voice:

> The session brought together a government department and Microsoft to work through zero-legacy for public sector. This note records what was covered, what emerged, and where it points next, so that those who were not in the room can pick up the thread.
>
> Three themes ran through the day. The first was the cost of carrying legacy estates that no longer serve the policy they were built for. [Each theme is then developed in prose: what was discussed, the evidence raised in the room, and what it implies.]
>
> The clearest signal from the session was that departments will move when the migration path is shown to be lower-risk than standing still, not before. That matters because it changes where the early effort should go. The immediate next steps are [...].

Note what is absent: no greeting, no sign-off, no "folk", no emoji, no spaced-hyphen mannerism. The voice is clean and professional; the structure carries the reader to understanding.

## Broadcast / framing register — the anchor exemplar

This is Andrew's authentic from-scratch instinct when he frames and brings people along (LinkedIn, 2014). It is the calibration target for the broadcast/framing register — **with one adjustment: today's senior voice keeps this instinct but is more economical and authoritative, so compress it hard.** Match the feel, not the length.

> *"It's easy to believe that some people were born in life to do what they do, that somehow they are gifted at birth or it's part of their DNA. The way that they make it look so easy, that their knowledge just flows from their lips or fingertips like it was gospel... In reality, when you see them in full flow of their 'expertise', it's a common mistake to forget or to not even realise how much time they have spent repeating over and over and over, through trial and error, through failure and success, through sacrifice.*
>
> *Is it our own mistake, or is that what we're taught to believe?... The truth is, anyone can achieve what they want in life by committing themselves to that outcome... For myself, I've learned that the only way to know, is to try. How do you know if you can, if you never try?*
>
> *...Even after the first few weeks, stop start, walking to running, I began to see an improvement. Small progress, small improvements, growing my ability... I was living proof that the only way to improve is to repeat, over and over and over."*

What to carry forward:
- **Common-ground open.** Starts from a shared, relatable observation, not a transactional hook — levels the room before making a point.
- **Flowing compound sentences that build and resolve.** No clipped fragments, no blunt openers.
- **Rhetorical questions** that draw the reader in ("How do you know if you can, if you never try?").
- **Rhythmic repetition as a device** ("over and over and over"; "small progress, small improvements, growing my ability").
- **A credible personal vantage** carrying a universal point; honest, never boastful.
- **Lands on something quietly earned** — reflective, not a portentous significance tail.
- Educated but accessible; brings people on the journey **without assuming where they are**.
